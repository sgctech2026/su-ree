THE SCIENCE

Why these molecules, at these doses.

Skincare claims are cheap. Below is the reasoning behind every active we use, the process that keeps them intact, and the testing we pay for.

Three actives. Each one earns its place.

We do not blend twenty ingredients and hope. Every active in a su-ree formula has a published mechanism, a dose we can defend, and a stability window we test against.

  • PDRN

    Polydeoxyribonucleotide, salmon-derived

    Fragments of DNA that bind the adenosine A2A receptor. In clinical work it is used to calm inflammatory signalling and support the skin's own repair cycle. We dose it at 2% in the pink beads.

  • GHK-Cu

    Copper tripeptide-1

    A naturally occurring peptide that declines sharply with age. It is one of the few actives with direct evidence for collagen I and III synthesis. Held at 0.05% — high enough to work, low enough to stay stable.

  • Beta-glucan

    Oat-derived, 1,3/1,6 chain

    A humectant that holds water in the upper layers without the sting that low-molecular hyaluronic acid can cause on compromised skin. It is what makes the cream feel cushioned rather than tacky.

How a jar gets made.

Four steps, and the fourth is the one most brands skip.

  1. 01

    Actives kept apart

    PDRN and copper peptides lose potency when they sit pre-mixed in one emulsion. Holding each in its own bead keeps them apart until you apply.

  2. 02

    Cold-fill at 24°C

    GHK-Cu denatures above roughly 40°C. Our filling line never exceeds 24°C, which rules out the hot-emulsion process most creams use.

  3. 03

    Lot-level assay

    Every batch is assayed for actives concentration and microbial load before release. The report is what your lot code opens.

  4. 04

    Batch report published

    The certificate is posted the day the batch ships. Nothing is held back, including batches that came in at the low end of our range.
